What does fiduciary mean?

Fiduciary derives from the Latin fidere — "to trust". The investment adviser (that's me) has the power and obligation to act for the beneficiary (that's you, the client) under circumstances that require total trust, good faith, and honesty.

In simple English: your interests come before mine.

Frequently asked questions

Why did you decide to become an investment adviser?

Three reasons.

For years I've made investment and financial-planning decisions for myself and my family, and it has worked pretty well. Now I want to put that experience to good use — to help you.

Second, I strongly believe being a fiduciary is the best way to help anyone. Just as doctors look after your health, a financial adviser looks after your financial well-being. More fiduciaries is good for consumers.

Third, it is difficult to find a fiduciary — and harder still to find a genuine adviser whose advice is tailored to your specific needs.

Are you always a fiduciary, and will you state that in writing?

Yes. My relationship with you is purely fiduciary. The client's best interest is the key driver of the relationship. There's no such thing as a part-time fiduciary — either one is in the fiduciary business, or not. And I'm in the fiduciary business.

Does anybody else pay you to recommend certain products or services?

No. That would violate SEBI regulations. I do not earn commissions on products and I do not sell products. To buy financial products suitable to you, I'll happily recommend good financial distributors or the "direct route".

What kind of advisory services do you offer?

I offer bespoke financial and investment advisory to accredited investors only.

Who is an accredited investor?

An accredited investor is an individual or entity that meets prescribed income/net-worth criteria and is therefore allowed to participate in certain high-risk, less-regulated investment opportunities (such as AIFs, PMS, and private placements) with lighter regulatory protections. This makes it easier for me to offer investment advice and keep my practice to a limited clientele whom I can serve to a high standard.

How much do you charge?

It depends on how complex the client's requirements are. It's usually between 1% and 2% of AuA — less than the fees charged by portfolio managers and fund houses. I have no employees; I do everything myself, so I expect to be compensated fairly.

Why don't you have employees?

Good question. First, I do not want to scale up — I'm choosy about my clientele and happy to stay small and nimble. It gives me breathing room for the other aspects of my life. Second, I like to be in control and make sure the client is served well; delegation invites slip-ups and second-order problems. Third, I perform best on my own. I love the freedom.

Are your fees negotiable?

No. I offer very good value for money.

What is the minimum threshold to be a client?

  • You need to be an Accredited Investor (AI) under SEBI regulations, with an AI certificate from a certifying agency. A quick search will turn up several; alternatively, ask your Chartered Accountant to help.
  • Willingness to take accountability and responsibility for decisions. While I'm here to handhold you, the responsibility is ultimately yours.
  • Willingness to pay fees.

Who manages your money?

I do — I manage my entire family's assets. It's reasonable to have assets managed by a third party to keep my biases in check, so I've diversified our portfolios across different asset managers, each with its own philosophy, while directly managing a portion myself. This twin approach refines my own thinking (which helps me serve clients) and keeps my skin in the game. Both are critical to understanding the nature of financial advice.

How do I contact you?

First, confirm you're an accredited investor and obtain the certification. Then email me at aditya.invest@outlook.com. Introduce yourself, tell me how you found me and why you want me as your adviser, and describe the issues you hope to solve — be specific. We'll talk over a beverage of your choice and decide whether we're a good fit.

Investor Charter in respect of Investment Advisers (IAs)

A. Vision and mission

Vision: Invest with knowledge and safety.

Mission: Every investor should be able to invest in the right investment products based on their needs, manage and monitor them to meet their goals, access reports, and enjoy financial wellness.

B. Details of business transacted by the Investment Adviser

  • Enter into an agreement with the client providing all details including fees, conflict-of-interest disclosure, and maintaining confidentiality of information.
  • Conduct proper and unbiased risk-profiling and suitability assessment of the client.
  • Conduct an audit annually.
  • Disclose the status of complaints on its website.
  • Disclose name, proprietor name, type of registration, registration number, validity, complete address with telephone numbers, and associated SEBI office details on its website.
  • Employ only qualified and certified employees.
  • Deal with clients only from the official number.
  • Maintain records of interactions with all clients, including prospective clients, where any advice-related conversation has taken place.
  • Ensure all advertisements adhere to the Advertisement Code for Investment Advisers.
  • Not discriminate between clients opting for the same or similar products/services.

C. Details of services provided to investors

  • Onboarding of clients; sharing of the agreement copy; completing client KYC.
  • Provide full disclosure about business, affiliations, and compensation in the agreement.
  • Not access a client's accounts or holdings for offering advice.
  • Disclose the risk profile to the client.
  • Disclose any conflict of interest between advisory activities and other activities.
  • Disclose the extent of use of Artificial Intelligence tools in providing advisory services.
  • Provide investment advice based on the risk-profiling and suitability of the client.
  • Treat all advisory clients with honesty and integrity.
  • Make adequate disclosure of all material facts such as risks, obligations, and costs relating to the products or securities advised.
  • Provide clear guidance and adequate caution when advising on complex and high-risk products/services.
  • Ensure confidentiality of client information unless required for legal obligations or with client consent.
  • Disclose timelines for various services and adhere to them.

D. Grievance redressal mechanism

Lodging complaints with the Investment Adviser: Investors may approach the Investment Adviser, who shall strive to redress the grievance immediately, but not later than 21 days of receipt.

Filing complaints on SCORES or with IAASB: SCORES 2.0 is SEBI's web-based centralised grievance redressal system (scores.sebi.gov.in). First review is by the designated body (IAASB); second review by SEBI.

If not satisfied: File a complaint on the SMARTODR platform for resolution through online conciliation or arbitration.

Physical complaints: Office of Investor Assistance and Education, Securities and Exchange Board of India, SEBI Bhavan, Plot No. C4-A, 'G' Block, Bandra-Kurla Complex, Bandra (E), Mumbai — 400 051.

E. Rights of investors

  • Right to privacy and confidentiality.
  • Right to transparent practices.
  • Right to fair and equitable treatment.
  • Right to adequate information.
  • Right to initial and continuing disclosure (including statutory and regulatory disclosures).
  • Right to fair and true advertisement.
  • Right to awareness about service parameters and turnaround times.
  • Right to be informed of the timelines for each service.
  • Right to be heard and to satisfactory grievance redressal.
  • Right to timely redressal.
  • Right to suitability of financial products.
  • Right to exit a financial product or service in accordance with the agreement.
  • Right to clear guidance and caution when dealing in complex and high-risk products and services.
  • Additional rights for vulnerable consumers (e.g., accessible service even if differently abled).
  • Right to provide feedback on the financial products and services used.
  • Right against coercive, unfair, and one-sided clauses in financial agreements.

F. Expectations from investors (responsibilities)

Do's

  • Always deal with SEBI-registered Investment Advisers.
  • Ensure the Investment Adviser has a valid registration certificate.
  • Check for the SEBI registration number (see the SEBI website for the list of registered IAs).
  • Pay only advisory fees, via banking channels; maintain signed receipts. You may pay via the Centralised Fee Collection Mechanism (CeFCoM) if available.
  • Always ask for your risk profiling before accepting advice; ensure advice is based on your risk profile and available alternatives.
  • Ask all relevant questions and clear doubts before acting on advice.
  • Assess risk–return profile, liquidity, and safety before investing.
  • Insist on written, signed, and stamped terms and conditions; read them carefully.
  • Be vigilant in your transactions.
  • Approach appropriate authorities for redressal of doubts/grievances.
  • Inform SEBI about IAs offering assured or guaranteed returns.
  • Be aware of your right to exit, seek clarifications, and provide feedback.

Don'ts

  • Don't fall for stock tips offered as investment advice.
  • Do not provide funds for investment to the Investment Adviser.
  • Don't fall for indicative, exorbitant, or assured returns; don't let greed override rational decisions.
  • Don't fall prey to luring advertisements or market rumours.
  • Avoid transactions based solely on phone calls or messages from IAs or their representatives.
  • Don't act just because of repeated messages or calls.
  • Don't fall for limited-period discounts, incentives, or gifts.
  • Don't rush into investments that don't match your risk appetite and goals.
  • Do not share login credentials or passwords of trading, demat, or bank accounts with the IA.
